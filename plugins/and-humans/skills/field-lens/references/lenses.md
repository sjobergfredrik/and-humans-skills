# Strategic Lenses — Reference

## Structural Lenses — the shape of the field

### Field Theory (Bourdieu)
What holds the structure in place? Which forms of capital dominate,
where is the center of gravity, and which actors lose from change?
Established fields pull toward the center — that's where norms
reproduce and margins shrink.

**Questions to ask:**
- Which forms of capital dominate this field? (economic, cultural, social, symbolic)
- Where is the center of gravity?
- Which actors lose the most if the field changes?

### Barbell / Extremes
Fields pull toward the poles — niche or scale. The middle shrinks.
Where are the actors today, and which direction are they moving?
Survivors are either so niche they define their own subfield, or so
large they *are* the field.

**Questions to ask:**
- Where are the actors — niche or scale?
- Is the middle shrinking? Who's stuck there?
- Which direction is the field moving?

### Wardley
Where on the evolution axis does the component sit — genesis, custom,
product, commodity? What should be built, bought, or abandoned?
Makes movement patterns and dependencies in the value chain visible.

**Questions to ask:**
- Where on the evolution axis does the core value sit?
- What dependencies are hidden?
- What's treated as custom that is actually commodity?

## Economic Lenses — what's unowned

### Externalities → Internalization
Find information flows or costs borne by a third party without
showing up in the price. Whoever builds the infrastructure for
redistribution defines a new pole — or creates a new field.

**Pattern:** noise → signal, friction → market, information asymmetry → infrastructure.

**CI example:** the actor's time at auditions is an externality the
producer doesn't pay for. Whoever makes it visible and redistributes
it shifts the power balance.

**Questions to ask:**
- What costs or flows are borne by a third party without showing up in the price?
- Where are the information asymmetries?
- Who would pay for transparency?

### First Principles (Musk)
Break down to fundamentals, rebuild from there. What do we take for
granted in this field that is actually a convention, not a necessity?

**Questions to ask:**
- What is convention vs necessity in this field?
- If you built this from scratch today, what would look different?

## Intentional Lenses — what the actor actually optimizes for

### The Paine Lens (Sarah Paine)
Don't read actions as sequences of decisions — read them as expressions
of deeper strategic logic on a longer timeline. What is the dominant
actor *actually* optimizing for? The structure is the result of
someone's intention.

**Questions to ask:**
- What is the dominant actor *actually* optimizing for?
- What strategic logic explains the last 3–5 years of moves?
- Is any actor playing a different game than what they claim?

### Fligstein & McAdam — Strategic Action Fields
A field is a social space where actors compete for resources,
recognition, and position. Fields are not static — they are the
result of ongoing negotiation.

**Core concepts:**
- **Incumbents vs challengers**: Dominant actors benefit from status
  quo. Challengers try to restructure. A new digital product is almost
  always a challenger.
- **Social skill**: The ability to mobilize cooperation through
  understanding others' mental states.
- **Settlement**: A stabilized field. Parties know the rules and
  stakes. Stability ≠ peace — it's frozen negotiation.
- **Rupture**: A settlement breaks from exogenous shocks (new tech,
  legislation) or skilled challengers who mobilize enough actors.
- **Nested fields**: Fields are embedded in each other like Russian
  dolls. Change in one field often triggers change in adjacent fields.

**Questions to ask:**
- Who is incumbent, who is challenger?
- Is the field in settlement or rupture?
- Which adjacent fields are affected if this field changes?
- What exogenous shock (if any) is underway right now?

## Design Lenses — form and intervention

### Virgil Abloh — The 3% Rule
What's the minimal change that shifts meaning entirely?
Avoids overengineering.

### Teenage Engineering — Constraint as Force
What constraint would make the product more distinct, not less?
The opposite of feature-driven development.

### Nothing (Carl Pei) — Readability as Claim
What does your product hide that it should show? Transparency
is not aesthetics — it's a promise to the user.

### Apple (Jobs) — Subtraction
What does the user think they want that is actually in the way?

**Shared questions for design lenses:**
- Where is the minimal intervention point?
- What constraint would sharpen, not limit?
- What should be subtracted?
- What is hidden that should be visible?

## Combinations

The lenses reinforce each other:
- **Bourdieu + Paine** = structure *and* intention
- **Barbell + Wardley** = where value sits *and* for how long
- **Externalities + first principles** = unowned flow *and* why it's
  still unowned
- **F&M + Externalities** = an externality that gets internalized is a
  rupture trigger
- **3% + F&M** = minimal intervention that breaks a settlement
